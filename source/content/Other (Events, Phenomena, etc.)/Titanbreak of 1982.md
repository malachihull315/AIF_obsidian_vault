
The titanbreak of 1982 was a titanbreak occurring in southwestern Germany on the 15th of March in 1982. The titan, later called Dedrick, was the first titan to be caught on camera and also the first titan to be captured and studied. This was the event that proved the existence of titans, and would later lead to the creation of the first Otherplane engine. 

## The Titanbreak
The titanbreak occurred at 1:30 PM, over the forest near the town of Freiburg im Breisgau in West Germany. Dedrick was a young titan, being only 36 metres long, but still caused significant damage to the surrounding environment, devouring a large swathe of the forest. The Tear was captured in the "Broken Lens" photograph taken at a wedding by Alice Habetler, and the presence of the titan would be immediately reported to the authorities. Freiburg remained almost entirely unharmed by the titan, and no civilian casualties or injuries were recorded during the titanbreak. After recieving word from the local police, the military sent a team to respond to the situation.

Newspapers and broadcasts worldwide would report the sighting of a "dragon" in Germany, several providing live coverage of the event. The 1982 titanbreak is one of the most covered events in recent history. A monument to the soldiers who died in the capture of Dedrick is located beneath the point where the Tear first happened, and remains a popular spot for hikers.

The restaurant where the Broken Lens photograph was taken has the original print of the picture hanging above the bar, and leverages its connection to the titanbreak in its marketing. It is a popular destination for blitzologists, with many famous figures in the field having visited the location.
## Capture
The capture of the pale titan was a slow and arduous process, as the soldiers performing it had none of the specialised equipment or procedures that would later become commonplace. Instead, Dedrick was continuously bombarded with artillery fire and rockets for several days. Although this didn't cause any damage to the titan, it eventually exhausted Dedrick to the point of collapse. This process took several days and was incredibly costly, with Dedrick finally succumbing to exhaustion at 5:49 AM, on the 19th of March. Casualties of the process were limited to soldiers, with 14 deaths and 9 serious injuries occurring throughout the attack. Dedrick was moved using a collection of trucks and tanks through the hills, where a facility would be quickly built to contain it.

Because of the lack of knowledge, the capture was completely improvised, and was the least efficient titan capture in recorded history. Modern titanbreak protocol calls for ascendant combatants to be dispatched to exhaust the titan, and the use of advanced restraints built to restrict the use of Tearing. This makes the process significantly easier and safer. 

## Research

The study of the titan quickly became of top priority, especially considering the unusual origins of the creature. Researchers from many countries were present at the titan's containment facility, with even a small group from the Soviet Union, despite political tensions. Although the countries were directly opposed, the West German government conceded and allowed the participation of the research team due to the importance of the discovery. The facility would later be expanded to house the large amount of participants on the project.

Dr Belle Greenway, a biologist from Ireland, gave the creature the title of "titan", and would later officially dub it the pale titan. The titan's origins were studied by allowing the titan limited access to food in order to provoke it to Tear, which gave insight into the Tears' nature as an entrance to another dimension. This other world was named the "Blitzwelt" by the German team, but American news companies decided to use the term "other plane", later leading to the most popular name "Otherplane". Some researchers at the facility decided to name the titan, eventually deciding on "Dedrick".

2 years later, this facility would be converted to the first Otherplane engine in cooperation with the newly renamed Halo Transport Corporation. It remains a hotspot for researchers even today.
